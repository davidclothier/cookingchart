A recipe has three parts: the ingredients, the steps and the dish you serve. Each step is a single line:

result = verb(inputs) duration

The verb comes from a shared vocabulary. boil is a stovetop verb (orange bar) and unattended by default: while it boils you can do something else. That is why the bar is hatched.

', 'Ingredients drop onto the step that uses them, with their quantity. The gold circle is the dish, with the total time.'), ('Chaining steps', '

First the water boils, then the pasta cooks, and finally it is drained. Just use the result of one step as an input of the next: boiling_water comes out of one step and goes into the other.

You don\'t write times or the order of the steps: the scheduler works them out from the names.

', 'The salt doesn\'t go in at the start: it drops in right when the pasta starts cooking, which is when it is used. Draining isn\'t hatched, because that one needs you.'), ('Something in parallel: the sauce', '

The sauce doesn\'t depend on the pasta, so it can be made at the same time. You don\'t say it is parallel: you just write it. The chart gives it its own lane and makes it flow into the step that uses it, mix.

One after another these steps would take 44 minutes; in parallel the recipe takes 25.

', 'The sauce sets the pace: it is the critical path (the white line under its bars). The pasta finishes earlier and waits: that is the dashed stretch.'), ('An ingredient halfway through', '

Not everything goes in at the start of a step. The basil is added when the sauce has been on the heat for 12 minutes. Write it with a + right below the step:

+ basil at 12min

The minute counts from the start of that step. It also works for something prepared in another step, not only for ingredients.

', 'The basil drops onto the simmering bar at its exact minute, not at the start.'), ('Tools and hands', '

So far the chart assumed endless hands. With cooks 1 you say a single person is cooking, and the scheduler won\'t let two steps that need your hands overlap.

We also declare the tools and where each step happens (in pot, with colander), plus a parameter like @heat:low. All of this is optional: you only write it if it matters to you.

', 'The Hands strip below the chart shows when you are busy: 11 of the 25 minutes. The heat and the water do the rest.'), ('What can\'t wait', '

In the previous step the pasta was done at minute 19 and waited for the sauce. Pasta that waits gets mushy or sticky. As the author, you say so with no wait on the cooked and the drained pasta.

The scheduler delays the pasta so it arrives right when the sauce is ready. If that were impossible (say, because your hands are busy at that moment), it wouldn\'t change the recipe: it would mark the wait in red so you can decide.

', 'Now the pasta starts at minute 12 and ends right before mixing. What waits is the boiling water, which doesn\'t spoil.'), ('Just in time', '

no wait protects what matters, but everything else still happens as soon as possible. If you\'d rather have everything finish right when it is needed, declare it with schedule just_in_time.

It is the recipe author\'s decision: the chart will be drawn this way wherever it is embedded. Readers can compare with the switch at the top right.

', 'The water no longer starts boiling at minute 0 but at minute 4: it boils right when the pasta goes in, and nothing waits anymore.'), ('Fine details', '
